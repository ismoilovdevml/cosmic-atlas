## What is it?

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and by far the largest. It is a gas giant: it has no solid
surface to stand on, only thick layers of hydrogen and helium that get denser and hotter with depth.
What we see in pictures are the tops of its clouds, arranged in light and dark bands that run around
the planet.

Jupiter is so massive that it shapes the rest of the Solar System. Its gravity tugs on asteroids,
comets and the orbits of the other planets. Its mass is about 318 times Earth's, and about 2.5 times
the mass of all the other planets combined.

## How do we know?

Jupiter's mass is measured from the orbits of its moons and of the spacecraft that have flown past
or orbited it. The faster a moon circles at a given distance, the heavier the planet must be. Its
size comes from how large it looks from a known distance and from spacecraft images. Dividing mass by
volume gives a density of only 1,326 kg/m³, a little more than water. Rocky Earth is about four
times denser, so Jupiter must be made mostly of the lightest elements, hydrogen and helium.

The day length was found by timing features that rotate in and out of view: Jupiter spins once in
just under ten hours. That fast spin flattens it: the radius at the equator is 71,492 km, but from
the centre to a pole it is only 66,854 km.

## Surprising but true

- Jupiter has the shortest day of any planet: about 9 hours 56 minutes.
- The Great Red Spot is a storm about twice as wide as Earth, and it has been watched for more than
  300 years.
- Galileo Galilei first saw Jupiter's four largest moons in 1610. One of them, Ganymede, is the largest
  moon in the Solar System and is even bigger than the planet Mercury.
- Jupiter has rings. They are faint and dusty, and were only discovered in 1979, by the Voyager 1
  spacecraft.
- Its magnetic field is 16 to 54 times stronger than Earth's, and its magnetic bubble reaches 1 to
  3 million km toward the Sun.
- New small moons keep being found: NASA counted 115 recognised by the IAU in 2026, and the number is
  still growing.
