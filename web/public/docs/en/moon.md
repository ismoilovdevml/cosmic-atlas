## What is it?

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It is a rocky world a little more than a quarter of
Earth's width, with no real air: its whole atmosphere weighs only about 25,000 kg. Without air to
spread heat around, the ground at the equator heats to about 117 °C by day and cools to about
−178 °C at night.

How the Moon formed is not known for certain. The leading hypothesis is that a Mars-sized body
collided with the young Earth, and the Moon formed after that impact. It remains a hypothesis: the
best explanation we have, not something anyone saw happen.

## Key numbers

| | Moon |
|---|---|
| Mean radius | 1,737.4 km (0.27 × Earth) |
| Mass | 7.35 × 10²² kg (0.0123 × Earth) |
| Mean distance from Earth | 384,400 km |
| Distance range | about 357,000–407,000 km |
| Orbit relative to the stars | 27.32 days |
| From new Moon to new Moon | 29.53 days |
| Surface gravity | 1.62 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's) |
| Temperature at the equator | −178 °C to +117 °C |
| Moving away from Earth | 3.8 cm per year |

## How do we know?

People have brought the Moon to the laboratory. Six Apollo missions landed between 20 July 1969
(Apollo 11) and December 1972 (Apollo 17), and together they returned almost 400 kg of lunar rock and
soil. These samples are still studied in laboratories on Earth today.

The Moon's mass is measured through gravity: through its pull on Earth and on spacecraft that orbit it.
Its distance is known so precisely that even its slow drift away from Earth, 3.8 cm a year, can be
measured. Scientists have also found high concentrations of
water ice in permanently shadowed craters near the lunar poles.

## Surprising but true

- We always see the same side of the Moon. It spins once in exactly the time it takes to go around
  Earth, 27.32 days, so the same half always faces us. This is called tidal locking.
- The Moon goes around Earth in 27.32 days, but from one full Moon to the next takes 29.53 days.
  The extra two days are needed because Earth has moved along its orbit around the Sun in the meantime.
- Light from the Moon reaches us in about 1.3 seconds.
- On the Moon you would weigh about one sixth of what you weigh on Earth.
- Twelve people have walked on the Moon: two astronauts from each of the six Apollo landings. It is the
  only world beyond Earth that people have visited.
- The Moon's distance changes during each orbit, from about 357,000 km to about 407,000 km. That is why
  some full Moons look a little bigger than others.
