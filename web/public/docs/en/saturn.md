## What is it?

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest, after Jupiter. Like Jupiter it is a
gas giant, made mostly of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface. It has been known since
prehistoric times, because it is easy to see with the naked eye.

What makes Saturn famous is its ring system. The rings are made of billions of small chunks of ice and rock
coated with dust, each one orbiting the planet like a tiny moon. They reach up to 282,000 km from the
planet, yet the main rings are typically only about 10 metres thick.

## How do we know?

Saturn's mass comes from the orbits of its moons and from the path of the Cassini spacecraft, which
orbited Saturn from 2004 until its mission ended on 15 September 2017. Its size comes from telescope and
spacecraft images. Divide the mass by the volume and you get a mean density of 687 kg/m³: Saturn is the only planet in the Solar System less dense than water.

Cassini spent thirteen years among the rings and moons, studying them from close up. Among its
discoveries were jets of water spraying from the small moon Enceladus, which hides an ocean under its
icy crust.

## Surprising but true

- Saturn spins so fast, once every 10 hours 39 minutes, that it is visibly squashed: its radius at the
  poles is 54,364 km, almost 6,000 km less than at the equator.
- The rings span hundreds of thousands of kilometres, but the main rings are mostly only about 10 m
  thick.
- Winds at Saturn's equator reach 500 metres per second, far faster than the strongest hurricanes on
  Earth.
- At Saturn's north pole the clouds form a six-sided pattern, a hexagon about 30,000 km across.
- Saturn has more known moons than any other planet: 293 as of August 2026, and more are still being
  found.
- A year on Saturn lasts 29.4 Earth years, so since Cassini arrived in 2004 Saturn has not yet
  finished a single trip around the Sun.
