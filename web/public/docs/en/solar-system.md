## What is it?

The Solar System is the Sun together with everything that orbits it. The Sun holds 99.8 % of all the
mass, so everything else, even giant Jupiter, is a small companion circling it. Closest to the Sun are
the four rocky planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars. After the [asteroid belt](doc:asteroid-belt)
come the four giants: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper belt, a ring of icy bodies. Pluto lives there. Since 2006 the
International Astronomical Union (IAU) calls Pluto a dwarf planet, and it recognises five of them:
Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris. Much farther out, comets are thought to come from a vast
shell of icy bodies called the Oort cloud.

## Key numbers

| | Solar System |
|---|---|
| Age (oldest solids in meteorites) | 4.567 billion years |
| Sun's share of the mass | 99.8 % |
| Planets / dwarf planets (IAU) | 8 / 5 |
| Known moons of the planets (as of 2026) | 456 |
| Mean distance of Neptune, the outermost planet | 4.5 billion km (30.2 au) |
| Light travel time from the Sun to Neptune | about 4.2 hours |
| Main part of the Kuiper belt | 39.4–47.8 au from the Sun |
| Oort cloud (theorised) | from 2,000–5,000 au out to 10,000–100,000 au |

One astronomical unit (au) is the mean Earth–Sun distance, about 150 million km; see
[units](doc:units).

## How do we know?

The age comes from meteorites. Some of them contain tiny grains that were the very first solids to
condense around the young Sun. Their uranium slowly decays into lead at a known rate, so measuring
the lead and uranium isotopes works like a clock. Those grains are 4.567 billion years old.

Distances and masses come from orbits. Since the time of Kepler and Newton, the motion of planets
and moons has told us how strongly each body pulls on the others. Today the distances to spacecraft are
measured very precisely by radio, and the same method weighs even the Kuiper belt: its total pull on the outer
planets shows that the whole belt has only about 2 % of Earth's mass.

The Oort cloud is different. Nobody has seen it directly. Its existence is inferred from the orbits
of long-period comets that fall in from all directions, so it remains a well-supported hypothesis
rather than an observed object.

## Surprising but true

- Jupiter alone has about 2.5 times the mass of all the other planets put together.
- Moon counts keep growing. NASA's pages, updated in September 2026, list 115 moons for Jupiter,
  293 for Saturn, 29 for Uranus and 16 for Neptune. Mercury and Venus have none.
- Sunlight that leaves the Sun now reaches Neptune about four hours later.
- The Sun does not stay put either: it carries the whole Solar System around the centre of the
  Milky Way once in about 230 million years.
- [Voyager 1](doc:voyager-1) crossed the edge of the Sun's bubble of solar wind, the heliopause, on
  25 August 2012, 121.6 au from the Sun. Even so, at its speed it needs about 300 more years to
  reach the inner edge of the Oort cloud, and perhaps 30,000 years to pass through it.
