## What is it?

A **galaxy** is a vast system of stars, planets, gas and dust, all held together by gravity. Galaxies
also contain invisible **dark matter**, known only from its pull. The largest galaxies hold
trillions of stars and span more than a million light-years; dwarf galaxies are far smaller.

Galaxies come in a few main shapes:

- **Spiral galaxies**, flat discs with spiral arms, like our [Milky Way](doc:milky-way)
  and the Andromeda Galaxy;
- **Elliptical galaxies**, smooth round or oval swarms of stars, such as the giant M87;
- **Irregular galaxies**, without a clear shape.

Most large galaxies have a supermassive [black hole](doc:black-hole) at their centre. Galaxies also
live together: in groups of up to about a hundred members, in clusters of thousands, and along the
filaments of the [cosmic web](doc:cosmic-web).

## How do we know?

A hundred years ago nobody knew whether the fuzzy "spiral nebulae" were inside the Milky Way or far
outside it. On the night of 5–6 October 1923, Edwin Hubble found a Cepheid variable star in the
Andromeda nebula. Cepheids pulse with a period that reveals their true brightness, so he could work
out the distance: hundreds of thousands of light-years, far beyond the Milky Way. Andromeda was a
galaxy of its own. The distance used in this atlas is 783 kpc, about 2.55 million light-years
(McConnachie 2012).

For distant galaxies, distances come from [redshift](doc:redshift). Surveys like [DESI](doc:desi-survey)
measure millions of them, which lets us map galaxies in 3D.

## Surprising but true

- One study (Conselice et al. 2016) estimated about 2 trillion galaxies in the observable universe,
  most of them too small and faint for any telescope to see. It is an extrapolation, not a count.
- Andromeda is visible to the naked eye on a dark night. When you see it, you see light that left it
  about 2.6 million years ago.
- Hubble's first distance to Andromeda, from one star, was about 1 million light-years; by the end of
  1924, with 12 Cepheids, he had 900,000 light-years. It was too small, but it settled the question.

## Where to find it in this atlas

This atlas has several galaxy layers:

- [DESI](#layer=galaxies-desi): 13.56 million galaxies and 1.58 million quasars with redshifts;
- [2MRS](#layer=galaxies-2mrs): 43,463 bright galaxies across almost the whole sky;
- [Cosmicflows-4](#layer=galaxies-cf4): 55,877 galaxies with redshift-independent distances (off by
  default);
- [Local Volume](#layer=local-volume): 868 nearby galaxies;
- [Local Group](#layer=local-group): our own group (see [Local Group](doc:local-group)).
