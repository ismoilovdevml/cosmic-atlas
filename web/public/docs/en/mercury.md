## What is it?

Mercury is the planet closest to the Sun and the smallest of the eight planets. It is a rocky world
covered in craters and has almost no air: what surrounds it is a very thin exosphere, practically a
vacuum. Without a blanket of air to hold heat, its surface swings between scorching days and freezing
nights.

Inside, Mercury has an unusually large metal core. Its iron-rich core has a radius of about 2,074 km, roughly 85 % of the
whole planet's radius. That is why this small planet is almost as dense as Earth. Like Earth, Mercury
has a global magnetic field.

## Key numbers

| | Mercury |
|---|---|
| Mean radius | 2,439.7 km (0.38 × Earth) |
| Mass | 3.30 × 10²³ kg (0.055 × Earth) |
| Mean density | 5,429 kg/m³ |
| Mean distance from the Sun | 57.9 million km (0.387 au) |
| Year (one orbit) | 88 Earth days |
| Rotation (one spin) | 58.6 Earth days |
| Day (sunrise to sunrise) | 176 Earth days |
| Temperature | up to 430 °C by day, down to −180 °C at night |
| Moons | none |

## How do we know?

Mercury's orbit has been followed for centuries, and its size comes from its disc seen through
telescopes and from spacecraft. Its mass is measured by the pull it exerts on passing and orbiting
spacecraft, including NASA's MESSENGER, whose data were also used to model Mercury's magnetic field.

Once mass and size are known, the density follows. A density of 5,429 kg/m³ in such a small planet
means that a large part of it must be heavy iron. Careful measurements of how Mercury's gravity pulls
on a spacecraft, combined with models of the interior, show how big that core is.

## Surprising but true

- Mercury spins exactly three times for every two trips around the Sun: 3 × 58.65 days = 175.9 days =
  2 × 87.97 days. Because of this, the time from one sunrise to the next is 176 Earth days, two whole
  Mercury years.
- It is the fastest planet. It races around the Sun at 47.4 km/s on average, faster than any other
  planet.
- Mercury's orbit is the most stretched of all the planets: its distance from the Sun changes from
  46.0 million km to 69.8 million km.
- Even this close to the Sun, Mercury may hold water ice. It would sit at the poles, inside deep craters
  whose floors are in permanent shadow.
- Long cliffs, some hundreds of kilometres long, cross the surface. They formed as the planet's
  interior cooled and Mercury contracted.
- Mercury is closer to the Sun than Venus, yet Venus is hotter: Mercury's mean temperature is 167 °C,
  Venus's 464 °C. The difference is Venus's thick atmosphere, see [Venus](doc:venus).
