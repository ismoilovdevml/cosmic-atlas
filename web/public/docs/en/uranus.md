## What is it?

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun. It is about four times wider than Earth and about
14.5 times heavier. Together with Neptune it is one of the two ice giants: 80 % or more of its mass is
a hot, dense fluid of "icy" materials (water, methane and ammonia) above a small rocky core. Methane
gas in its atmosphere absorbs red sunlight, which gives Uranus its blue-green colour.

Its most unusual feature is its tilt. Uranus's axis is tipped by almost 98°, so the planet rolls
around the Sun on its side. It also spins "backwards", in the opposite direction to most planets.
Venus is the only other planet that does this.

## How do we know?

Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope. William Herschel found it on 13 March 1781;
before that, people knew only the planets visible to the naked eye. Its mass is measured from the
orbits of its moons, and its size from images.

Almost everything we know up close comes from a single visit. NASA's Voyager 2 flew past Uranus in
1986, and no spacecraft has been back since. The length of its day, 17.24 hours, was measured by
Voyager 2's radio experiment from the rotation of the planet's magnetic field. Uranus also has the coldest atmosphere of any planet: its minimum
temperature is 49 K, or −224 °C.

## Surprising but true

- Because Uranus lies on its side, for nearly a quarter of each 84-year orbit the Sun shines almost
  straight down on one pole, while the other half of the planet sits in a dark winter that lasts
  21 years.
- Uranus is not the farthest planet, yet its air reaches lower temperatures than Neptune's: the
  coldest planetary atmosphere measured is on Uranus.
- Its moons are not named after Greek or Roman gods, like those of most planets, but after characters
  in the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope.
- Uranus has rings too: 13 faint ones.
- Sunlight needs about 2 hours 40 minutes to reach Uranus, compared with about 8 minutes to reach
  Earth.
- One Uranus year is 84 Earth years. Since Herschel's discovery in 1781, Uranus has gone around the
  Sun only about three times.
