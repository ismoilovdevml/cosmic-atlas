## What is it?

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the largest of the four rocky planets. It is the only place
we know of that is inhabited by living things. About 71 % of its surface is covered by the global
ocean, and the air near the ground is 78 % nitrogen and 21 % oxygen.

Earth formed about 4.5 billion years ago, together with the rest of the
[Solar System](doc:solar-system). Its molten metal core and its rotation produce a global magnetic
field: it makes compass needles point north, and the charged particles of the solar wind trapped in it
light up the auroras.

## How do we know?

Earth's mass is weighed with its own gravity. Satellites and the [Moon](doc:moon) orbit Earth at known
distances and speeds, and their motion tells us how strongly Earth pulls, and therefore how massive it
is. Divide that mass by Earth's volume and you get a mean density of 5,514 kg/m³, the highest of all
the planets. Surface rocks are much lighter than that, so the inside must hold something heavier:
a core of metal.

The power of the greenhouse effect can be estimated with simple physics. With the sunlight Earth
absorbs, a planet without an atmosphere would settle at about 254 K, that is −19 °C. Earth's real
mean temperature is 15 °C. The difference of about 34 degrees is the warming from gases such as water
vapour and carbon dioxide; carbon dioxide is now about 420 parts per million of the dry air.

## Surprising but true

- A day of 24 hours is not quite one spin. Relative to the stars Earth turns once in 23 hours
  56 minutes. Because Earth also moves along its orbit, it needs about four more minutes to bring the
  Sun back to the same place in the sky.
- Seasons come from the tilt, not from distance. Earth's axis is tilted by 23.44°, so each hemisphere
  leans towards the Sun for part of the year. Earth is actually closest to the Sun (147.1 million km)
  in early January, during the northern winter.
- Earth is the densest planet in the Solar System, denser even than iron-rich Mercury (5,429 kg/m³).
- Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 19 seconds to reach us. Above the atmosphere, every square metre
  facing the Sun receives 1,361 watts.
- Earth's Moon is slowly drifting away, by about 3.8 cm per year.
