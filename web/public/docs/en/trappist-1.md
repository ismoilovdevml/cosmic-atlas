## What is it?

TRAPPIST-1 is a very small, cool red dwarf star 40.5 light-years away. It has only about 9 % of the
Sun's mass and is barely larger than the planet Jupiter. Its surface temperature is about 2,566 K and
it gives off less than a thousandth of the Sun's light.

What makes it famous is its family: seven rocky [exoplanets](doc:exoplanet), named b, c, d, e, f, g and
h in order from the star. All seven are roughly Earth-sized. Three were announced in 2016 and the other
four in 2017.

The whole system is tiny. Even the outermost planet, h, orbits at only 0.062 au, so all seven would fit
comfortably inside Mercury's orbit (0.387 au). Planet b goes round the star in about 36 hours.

radii), 2,566 ± 26 K. Distance: 12.43 pc (40.5 light-years).

## How do we know?

All seven planets pass in front of their star as seen from Earth. Each passage, called a transit, blocks
a little of the starlight. The depth of the dip gives the planet's size compared with the star.

The masses come from timing. The planets pull on one another, so each one arrives at its transit a bit
early or a bit late. The Spitzer Space Telescope recorded transit times for four years, and the
pattern of these small shifts reveals how heavy each planet is, to a precision of 3 to 5 %. Measuring the
same masses by the star's wobble would require detecting changes of just 2.5 cm/s in its speed.

With sizes and masses known, we get densities. All seven fit a single rocky recipe that is somewhat
poorer in iron than Earth (about 21 % iron by mass instead of 32 %), or Earth-like rock with extra light
material such as a surface water layer.

## Surprising but true

- Planets e, f and g receive between 0.65 and 0.25 times the starlight Earth gets from the Sun. Mars
  gets 0.43. So these three sit roughly in the range where liquid water could exist, if they have the
  right atmospheres.
- Whether any TRAPPIST-1 planet has an atmosphere is still an open question. The James Webb Space
  Telescope measured heat from the day sides of b and c. Planet b looks like bare rock with little or no
  air. Planet c, at about 380 K on its day side, has no thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere. The outer
  planets have not yet given a clear answer.
- The orbits form a near-resonant chain: their periods are close to ratios of small whole numbers, a sign
  the planets probably formed farther out and moved inward. The orbits are almost perfectly flat and
  stable for at least 10 million years in simulations.
- The star may be older than the Sun: one estimate puts its age at 7.6 ± 2.2 billion years.
