## What is it?

Proxima b is an [exoplanet](doc:exoplanet), a planet orbiting a star other than the Sun. Its star,
Proxima Centauri, is the Sun's closest stellar neighbour, 4.24 light-years away, so Proxima b and its
sibling Proxima d are the closest known planets outside our Solar System.

Proxima Centauri is a red dwarf: a small, cool star with about 12 % of the Sun's mass, a surface
temperature of about 3,050 K and only about 0.1 % of the Sun's luminosity. Because the star is so dim,
a planet must huddle very close to it to stay warm. Proxima b circles at only 0.0485 au, about an eighth
of Mercury's distance from the Sun, and its year lasts just 11.2 days. At that distance it receives
enough warmth that, in principle, water could be liquid on its surface.

## How do we know?

Nobody has seen Proxima b directly. It was found from the way it tugs on its star. As the planet
orbits, the star moves in a small circle too, alternately toward and away from us. That motion shifts
the star's spectral lines slightly blue, then slightly red (the Doppler effect). This is the
radial-velocity method. For Proxima b the star's speed changes by only about 1.2 metres per second,
slower than walking pace, repeating every 11.2 days.

The size of that wobble tells us the planet's mass, but only partly. We see only the part of the motion
along our line of sight. If the orbit is tilted, the true motion is bigger. So the method gives a
minimum mass, written m sin i, where i is the tilt of the orbit. Proxima b has at least about 1.06
Earth masses; its true mass could be larger. Its size has not been measured.

The first estimate in 2016 was 1.27 Earth masses. Better spectrographs, ESPRESSO in 2022 and NIRPS in
2025, refined it to 1.07 and then 1.055 Earth masses.

## Surprising but true

- Proxima has at least one more planet. Proxima d, with at least 0.26 Earth masses and a 5.12-day
  orbit, was a candidate in 2022 and was confirmed in 2025. A third signal, "Proxima c", with a period
  of about 1,900 days, remains unconfirmed: the 2025 analysis found the evidence inconclusive.
- Being in the "habitable zone" does not mean a planet is habitable. Proxima is a moderately active
  star, and nobody knows whether Proxima b has an atmosphere or water. This is an open question.
- At Voyager 1's speed of about 16.9 km/s, a trip to Proxima would take about 75,000 years.
- The light you would see from Proxima tonight left the star 4.24 years ago.
