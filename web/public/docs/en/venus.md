## What is it?

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the planet that comes closest to Earth. In size and
mass it is almost Earth's twin: its radius is 95 % of Earth's and its mass 82 %. But conditions on its
surface could hardly be more different.

Venus is wrapped in a thick atmosphere that is 96.5 % carbon dioxide. At the ground the pressure is
92 times that at sea level on Earth, like being more than 930 m deep in the ocean. This atmosphere
traps heat so well that the surface stays at about 464 °C, hot enough to melt lead. High above,
the planet is hidden by clouds of sulfuric acid.

## How do we know?

The clouds hide the surface from ordinary telescopes, so most of what we know came from spacecraft.
The Soviet Venera programme sent probes between 1961 and 1984, and ten of them reached the surface.
They measured the heat and the pressure directly. None lasted long: the shortest survived 23 minutes,
the longest about two hours, before the conditions destroyed them.

Radar can see through clouds. NASA's Magellan spacecraft used radar to map the surface from orbit until
its mission ended in 1994. These maps show valleys and high mountains dotted with thousands of
volcanoes.

## Surprising but true

- On Venus a day is longer than a year. One spin takes 243 Earth days, one orbit only 224.7.
- Venus spins backwards compared with Earth and most other planets. Seen from its surface, the Sun
  would rise in the west and set in the east, and one sunrise to the next takes 116.75 Earth days.
- Venus is not the closest planet to the Sun, yet it is the hottest. Mercury, closer in, averages
  167 °C, while Venus averages 464 °C, because its carbon-dioxide air acts as a powerful greenhouse.
- Its clouds reflect about three-quarters of the sunlight that hits them (Bond albedo 0.77). That is
  why Venus is the third brightest object in our sky after the Sun and the Moon.
- No other planet comes closer to Earth: at its nearest, Venus is 38.2 million km away.
- Venus has no moons and, unlike Earth, no global magnetic field.
