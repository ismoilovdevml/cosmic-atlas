## What is it?

Most spacecraft stay near the Earth or orbit other planets. Five have been sent so fast that the Sun's
gravity can never pull them back. They are slowly heading out into interstellar space:

| Probe | Launched | Main targets | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pioneer 10 | 2 March 1972 | Jupiter (1973) | last signal 23 January 2003 |
| Pioneer 11 | 6 April 1973 | Jupiter (1974), Saturn (1979) | last contact 1995 |
| Voyager 2 | 20 August 1977 | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | active, extended mission |
| Voyager 1 | 5 September 1977 | Jupiter (1979), Saturn (1980) | active, extended mission |
| New Horizons | 19 January 2006 | Pluto (2015), Arrokoth (2019) | active |

## How do we know where they are?

Engineers track the probes by radio. The time a signal takes to go out and come back gives the
distance; the tiny change in its frequency (the Doppler shift) gives the speed. NASA's Jet Propulsion
Laboratory turns these measurements into precise positions, published through its Horizons system.

How do we know a probe has left the Sun's bubble? The Sun blows a wind of charged particles that
carves a bubble in space, the heliosphere. Its edge is the **heliopause**. On 25 August 2012, Voyager 1
saw a sudden and lasting change in the particles around it. Later, when a solar outburst made the
surrounding gas "ring", its plasma wave instrument showed that the gas was more than 40 times denser
than inside the heliosphere: Voyager 1 was in interstellar space. Voyager 2 followed in 2018.

## Surprising but true

- Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft ever to visit Uranus (1986) and Neptune (1989).
- On 21 August 2024 Voyager 1 was 164.7 au from Earth, moving at 17.0 km/s relative to the Sun. NASA
  expects it to reach a distance of one **light-day** from Earth on 18 November 2026: a radio signal will
  then take a full day to reach it.
- Pioneer 10's last signal, in 2003, took 11 hours and 20 minutes to reach Earth from 12.23 billion km.
  It is heading in the general direction of the red star Aldebaran, which it would pass in about two
  million years.
- New Horizons left Earth at about 58,536 km/h, the highest launch speed of any human-made object
  relative to Earth.
- Pioneer 10 carries a plaque, and the Voyagers carry golden records, as messages for anyone who
  might find them (see [interstellar message](doc:interstellar-message)).

## Where to find it in this atlas

The probes appear near the Sun, at the scale of the Solar System (around 100 au). Fly to
[Voyager 1](#focus=Voyager%201) ([article](doc:voyager-1)), [Voyager 2](#focus=Voyager%202),
[Pioneer 10](#focus=Pioneer%2010), [Pioneer 11](#focus=Pioneer%2011) and

switches to light-hours, then light-days. Even Voyager 1, after almost 50 years, is still less than a
thousandth of the way to the nearest star, Proxima Centauri (1.30 pc, about 268,000 au).
