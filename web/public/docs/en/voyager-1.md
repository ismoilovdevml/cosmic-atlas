## What is it?

Voyager 1 is a NASA space probe launched on 5 September 1977. It was built to fly past Jupiter and
Saturn, and it did: it passed Jupiter on 5 March 1979 and Saturn on 12 November 1980. After that it
kept going, out of the planetary region and away from the Sun, and it has never stopped.

On 25 August 2012 Voyager 1 became the first spacecraft to leave the heliosphere, the huge bubble
blown by the solar wind. At that moment it was 121.6 au from the Sun, about 120 times farther than
Earth. Since then it has been flying through interstellar space, the thin gas between the stars. It is
the most distant object ever made by people.

| Power source | 3 radioisotope generators, about 158 W each at launch |

## How do we know?

Voyager 1 still talks to Earth through NASA's Deep Space Network, a set of large radio dishes. The
time a radio signal takes to get there and back, and the small shift in its frequency caused by the
probe's motion, tell engineers how far away it is and how fast it moves. From such tracking JPL
computes its path; the numbers above come from JPL's Horizons system for 13 November 2026.

Voyager 1's distance grows by about 17 km every second, so any figure for it is only true for one
date. Its distance from Earth also wobbles a little through the year, because Earth itself moves around
the Sun, sometimes towards the probe and sometimes away from it.

## Surprising but true

- Voyager 1 is about to become one light-day away. NASA expects it to reach a distance of one light-day
  from Earth on 18 November 2026: a radio command sent from Earth then needs a full 24 hours to arrive,
  and the reply another 24 hours to come back.
- It carries the Golden Record, a gold-plated copper disc with greetings in 55 languages, 35 sounds of
  Earth, 90 minutes of music and 115 images, in case anyone ever finds it.
- From 40 au away, Voyager 1 took a "family portrait" of six planets. Its image of Earth, a tiny dot
  of light, inspired Carl Sagan's famous "Pale Blue Dot".
- On its way past Jupiter in 1979 it discovered that Jupiter has rings.
- Its power comes from the heat of decaying radioactive material, and that heat slowly fades. To save
  power, NASA has been switching off its instruments one by one.
- Even at 17 km/s, the journey is slow on a cosmic scale. Voyager 1 will need about 300 years just to
  reach the inner edge of the Oort cloud, the distant shell of icy bodies around the Sun. A craft
  moving at the same speed would need about 75,000 years to cover the distance to the nearest star,
  Proxima Centauri.
