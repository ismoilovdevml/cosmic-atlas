## What is it?

The Sun is a star, and the only star close enough to show us its surface in detail. Astronomers
classify it as a G2 V star: a star in the long, stable middle part of its life, when it shines by fusing hydrogen into helium in its core.

It is a ball of hot gas, mostly hydrogen and helium. At the visible surface, about 91 of every 100
atoms are hydrogen and almost 9 are helium. Every second the Sun turns about 4.26 million tonnes of
its mass into energy, and that energy, after a long journey out of the interior, leaves the surface
as light. The Sun holds 99.8 % of all the mass in the [Solar System](doc:solar-system).

## How do we know?

The Sun's mass comes from Earth's orbit. Earth goes around the Sun once a year at a known distance,
and gravity tells us how massive the Sun must be to hold it on that path. The same calculation works
for every planet, and they all give the same answer.

Its power comes from a simple measurement. Above the atmosphere, each square metre facing the Sun
receives 1,361 watts. Imagine a giant sphere around the Sun with a radius of 1 au: multiply 1,361 W
by that sphere's area and you get about 3.83 × 10²⁶ W, the Sun's total output.

The surface temperature follows from the power and the size: a ball of 695,700 km radius must be about
5,772 K to radiate that much. The temperature at the centre cannot be measured directly. It comes
from physical models of the Sun's interior, which have to match its mass, size, power and age.

## Surprising but true

- The light you see from the Sun left it about 8 minutes and 19 seconds ago. That is how long light
  needs to cross the 149.6 million km between us.
- The Sun does not spin like a solid ball. Its gas rotates faster at the equator than near the poles,
  so astronomers quote the rotation period at a chosen latitude: 25.4 days at 16°.
- The thin outer atmosphere, the corona, is far hotter than the surface below it: up to about
  2 million °C compared with roughly 5,500 °C.
- The Sun's activity rises and falls in a cycle of about 11 years, which we see as more or fewer
  sunspots.
- The Sun is a little less than halfway through its life. In about 5 billion years it will swell into
  a red giant and then shrink into a [white dwarf](doc:white-dwarf).
- The Sun also travels: it carries the whole Solar System around the centre of the Milky Way once in
  about 230 million years.
